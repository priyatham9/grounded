When does a reassuring metric conceal an unreliable decision?

A rate passes a screen. An answer passes a rubric. A model fits. A mapping exports. Each is a useful check. Each leaves a different question unanswered.

Follow the missing check.

Four investigations move from a summary to the evidence behind it—and to the review a decision still needs.

Read the findings ↓

A shared question, four different kinds of evidence. These results are not pooled, and they do not establish a universal failure rate. One examines administrative records; one exhausts a finite answer space; one simulates known truths; one tests what a representation preserves.

01Administrative records

A plausible ratio can hide an influential scale.

Empirical influence audit

Open the decision audit ↗︎

Two 2023 filings pass all {{ehs_screens}} tested hours-per-employee screens, yet account for {{ehs_hours_share}} of that year’s default-screened hours. Excluding them reverses the sign of the 2023–24 rate change.

A symmetric sensitivity removing the two largest-hour filings in both years gives {{ehs_symmetric}}. The selected pair is an influential example, not a claim that two is the minimum needed to change the sign.

What the audit establishes. Internal ratio consistency does not establish absolute-scale validity. Scaling both hours and employee counts together preserves their ratio.

What it leaves unknown. These are descriptive administrative filings, not a national estimate. The exclusion is a sensitivity analysis, not a corrected rate: it does not identify which reported fields, if any, are wrong.

Before using the trendReview the influential records and show the sensitivity alongside the aggregate.

The question asking for eight pictograms also accepts unrelated excerpts about eight-hour noise exposure and fatality-reporting intervals. The lexical key recognizes the number but not what it refers to. A scorer pass is not semantic validation.

What the audit establishes. {{ai_recoverable}} current misses have a passing indexed excerpt available; the other {{ai_unreachable}} cannot pass without changing the answer space or rubric.

What it leaves unknown. No language model was evaluated. The bound does not apply to full documents, multiple excerpts or synthesis. Lexical omissions may be missing information or unrecognized wording; this analysis does not adjudicate them.

Before comparing systemsCheck what the evidence can answer, then inspect what the scorer calls success.

03Known simulated truth

Good fit does not calibrate a contrast test.

Simulation · no workplace observations

Open the model audit ↗︎

At N={{sem_n}} and loading 0.75, {{sem_fit}}/{{sem_reps}} null simulations pass the fit criteria. The repository’s two-step estimator with a naïve analytic standard error still rejects a true null {{sem_false}} of the time, against its nominal 5% level.

The fit criteria are CFI ≥ 0.95, RMSEA ≤ 0.06 and SRMR ≤ 0.08. The two-step estimator treats estimated latent correlations as fixed in its naïve uncertainty calculation. These error rates do not describe all SEM estimators. Naïve-rate intervals are pointwise Wilson intervals; calibrated-rate intervals also include cutoff uncertainty through two-sample resampling. Five normal latent variables; four correlated predictors (ρ=0.35); independent measurement errors. Separate sets of 1,000 surveys estimate the null cutoff, test it under the null, and measure rejection under the alternative for each design cell.

For the N={{sem_n}} alternative contrast Δ=0.15, the null-calibrated diagnostic rejects in {{sem_alt}} of simulations (95% two-sample Monte Carlo interval {{sem_alt_low}}–{{sem_alt_high}}). The interval includes uncertainty in the estimated cutoff. It is not a population confidence interval.

What the audit establishes. In this specified simulation, fit checks do not protect the contrast test from underestimated uncertainty. A high rejection rate under an alternative is not useful power evidence until false positives are checked.

What it leaves unknown. The null-calibrated cutoff uses the known data-generating process. It is a diagnostic, not a correction available from real workplace data. Missingness, nonnormality and misspecified measurement were not simulated here.

Before ranking effectsCheck the inferential procedure under a known null, separately from model fit.

04Representation contract

All mappings can survive while recorded knowledge disappears.

Loss audit · author-proposed alignments

Open the translation audit ↗︎

The SKOS export preserves {{ont_mappings}}/{{ont_mappings}} PIF mapping expressions, but drops all {{ont_absences}} explicit “no counterpart” assertions and all {{ont_gaps}} external coverage-gap records.

Inspect what the round-trip test proves

Deleting the source’s explicit Workplace Visibility absence changes the source meaning while leaving the standard SKOS export unchanged. The export cannot distinguish those two source states.

A sidecar preserves the omitted assertion records. Reconstructing the declared contract recovers {{ont_triples}}/{{ont_triples}} scoped source triples, with zero missing and zero added. This is not full ontology reconstruction, semantic equivalence or validation of the mappings themselves.

What the audit establishes. A missing edge and an explicit statement of no counterpart are different. Mapping-count completeness alone cannot show that the export preserves that distinction.

What it leaves unknown. These alignments are author-proposed. Preserving their source statements does not establish their correctness, cross-framework equivalence, or any relationship to injury risk.

Before sharing a knowledge representationSpecify which assertions must survive, and test their round trip—not just edge counts.

From a result to a decision

Make the next review specific.

Choose a decision. The right audit depends on what you are about to infer.

  1. Before the decision

    Name the population, period and exposure denominator behind the rate.

  2. Run the audit

    Rank record influence, vary the screen, and show the trend with and without the influential filings.

  3. Review what remains

    Inspect source records before attributing a change to safety performance. Keep sensitivity separate from corrected truth.

Evidence that can be revisited

A common discipline.
Distinct claims.

The programme’s proposal is methodological: attach a decision to the quantity it needs, test a failure that the summary cannot reveal, and keep the remaining uncertainty visible. The four investigations supply different evidence for that practice. They do not estimate how often it succeeds across organisations.

These are working investigations, not externally peer-reviewed decision rules. Existing working papers, methods ledger and reproduction guide remain available.
