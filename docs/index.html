An ontology-based architecture whose conclusions carry derivation traces, an open benchmark for measuring whether an answer is bound to an authoritative source, and an empirical account of the data-quality problems underneath both.

Benchmarks built on raw OSHA data are wrong by this factor

2.24% of establishment filings fail a basic plausibility test, yet those filings carry 86.9% of every hour reported. Aggregate TRIR reads 0.45 as published and 3.41 once implausible records are excluded. The error flatters every organisation that uses it.

Source · OSHA ITA, CY2023–CY2025
38%
Zero is not excellence

In chemical manufacturing (NAICS 325), 38% of establishments report no recordable case at all while the 90th percentile sits at 5.54. Injury-rate distributions spike at zero rather than forming a bell curve, so reporting a mean against them is meaningless.

Source · OSHA ITA, NAICS 325
47.8%
Injuries cluster early in the shift

Across 546,590 cases recording both shift start and incident time, injuries peak in hour 2 and 47.8% occur within the first four hours — not at the fatigued end of the day, where fatigue-management programmes usually aim.

Source · OSHA case-level data

The failure that motivates the work

An ungrounded assistant asked about rupture disks returned pressure relief valve data instead. Semantically adjacent, operationally wrong. This is the structurally expected behaviour of retrieval with no authoritative binding, not an unlucky sample.

Contextual human-error risk factors formalised as a machine-readable ontology, with an explicit crosswalk to established Performance Shaping Factor frameworks — SPAR-H, CREAM, HFACS, HSE PIFs. The paper

Grounded Reasoning for Safety-Critical AI

An ontology-based architecture and evaluation benchmark for human error risk assessment in process industries.

The argument runs in four parts: that ungrounded retrieval fails structurally rather than incidentally in safety-critical technical queries; that contextual human-error factors can be formalised as an auditable ontology mapped honestly onto forty years of prior Performance Shaping Factor work; that grounding can be measured rather than asserted; and that the data quality underneath all of it determines whether any of it holds.

Target venues: Process Safety Progress, Journal of Loss Prevention in the Process Industries, AIChE Global Congress on Process Safety.

Status

Draft — not yet submitted

This page describes work in progress and states plainly what has and has not been established. The distinction matters more here than usual: the subject is whether AI systems overclaim, and a page that overclaimed about them would be self-refuting.

  • The benchmark contributes apparatus and a question corpus. A full grounded-versus-ungrounded experimental run is future work.
  • Reference verification is incomplete. Sources confirmed so far are canonical to their fields; the remainder are unverified and are being checked before submission.
  • No external peer review has been conducted.
  • Empirical findings above come from real public OSHA data. Where synthetic data is used in the repositories it is confined to simulation studies, kept in a synthetic/ directory with its generator, and never reported as a finding about the world.
