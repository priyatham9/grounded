Four investigations examine what a reassuring check leaves out. Empirical records, an exhaustive scorer diagnostic, controlled simulation and a representation test provide different evidence. They are not pooled into a universal failure rate.

01 / Empirical influence audit · OSHA administrative filings

One screened record can reverse a trend.

The default-screened 2023–24 injury-rate change is +15.73%. Omitting one influential 2023 filing changes it to -1.30%. This is a review sensitivity, not a corrected estimate.

TRIR = 200,000 × recordable cases / reported hours. Rates rounded to four decimals.
Scenario2023 rate2024 rateChange
Default-screened population3.14663.6415+15.73%
Without filing 21561213.68943.6415-1.30%

All 774,183 individual deletions across both years were tested; 1 reverses the sign. The other 7 adjacent-year comparisons have no single-deletion reversal. The candidate reports 14.37 billion hours and 7 cases.

What the check misses

Scaling hours and employees together preserves hours per employee while changing the injury rate. Internal ratio consistency cannot establish absolute-scale validity.

Practical review step

Apply the same leave-one-out influence rule to both years. Inspect flagged source records and retain them pending review; do not automatically delete them.

Limits. Retrospective analysis of administrative filings, not a probability sample or causal study. Influence does not identify an erroneous field or recover its true value. The rule was not historically preregistered.

02 / Exhaustive scorer diagnostic · No language model evaluated

Audit the answer space
before changing the retriever.

Testing every indexed excerpt against every factual question—4,284 combinations—finds any scorer-passing response for only 27/63 questions. BM25 already passes 18.

A pass worth disputing: the number has the wrong referent

Question: How many pictograms are designated under 29 CFR 1910.1200 for application to a hazard category?

Unrelated source excerpt: “an 8-hour time-weighted average of 85 decibels or a dose of fifty percent shall also be referred to as the action level”

The numeric-only key accepts this unrelated noise-exposure excerpt. A lexical pass is not semantic validation; this is not a language-model response.

What the score mixes

Score differences mix retrieval, the response format and lexical matching. None isolates system capability.

Practical review step

Measure what the available evidence can make the scorer accept, then audit accepted answers for meaning before attributing a score gap to system capability.

Limits. The ceiling is conditional on this corpus, format and rubric; it does not bound semantic answerability or synthesis. No language model was evaluated. Metadata can supply legitimate answer content.

03 / Controlled simulation · Known generating model

Good model fit does not
calibrate a contrast test.

At N=770 and loading 0.75, 1000/1000 null simulations pass the fit criteria. The repository’s two-step estimator with its naïve analytic standard error still rejects a true null in 17.9% of simulations.

Independent sets of 1,000 surveys per stage. Alternative contrast Δ=0.15.
ProcedureNull rejection (Δ=0)Alternative rejection
Naïve analytic test179/1000 (17.9%)727/1000 (72.7%)
Null-calibrated diagnostic50/1000 (5.0%)462/1000 (46.2%)
Oracle: latent scores observed50/1000 (5.0%)794/1000 (79.4%)

The naïve null rate has pointwise 95% Wilson interval 15.6%–20.4%. The calibrated alternative rate has 95% two-sample Monte Carlo interval 39.6%–50.2%, including cutoff uncertainty. High alternative rejection is not useful power evidence before false positives are checked.

What the fit check misses

The naïve uncertainty calculation treats estimated latent correlations as fixed. Fit criteria—CFI ≥ .95, RMSEA ≤ .06, SRMR ≤ .08—test a different property from contrast-test calibration.

Practical review step

Evaluate the contrast procedure on separate null and alternative samples. Keep measurement estimation in the uncertainty calculation; do not use fit alone to justify effect rankings.

Limits. Five normal latent variables, four correlated predictors (ρ=.35), three indicators per factor and independent errors; no missingness or workplace observations. Error rates are estimator-specific. The null-calibrated cutoff knows the data-generating process and is a diagnostic, not a real-data remedy. Only the completed primary experiment is summarized here.

04 / Representation-loss audit · Author-proposed crosswalk

A complete mapping count
can miss explicit absences.

The standard SKOS export retains 90/90 PIF mapping expressions, but omits all 6 explicit no-counterpart assertions and all 7 coverage-gap records.

Two source states, one identical export

WorkplaceVisibility → noCounterpartIn → SPAR-H

The source can assert no counterpart or say nothing. Deleting this absence changes the source but leaves its standard SKOS export byte-identical.

What a scoped repair proves

A separate RDF sidecar recovers 600/600 source triples in the declared crosswalk-record contract: 0 missing and 0 added. The standard SKOS graph remains unchanged.

Practical review step

Declare which assertions, explicit absences and provenance must survive an interchange. Test their reconstruction; do not use edge counts as the fidelity criterion.

Limits. A lossless scoped round trip does not reconstruct the entire ontology or validate the proposed alignments. It establishes no new taxonomy, causal relationship or prediction of injury risk.

A common review discipline, not a pooled empirical claim.Name the quantity. Challenge what the summary cannot test. Trace the evidence. Record what remains unknown.
